## 1. Canon — the truth under everything
1. **The Ledger runs a Plan.** Meridian's pre-Burn municipal intelligence survived air-gapped. Since Year 60
   (the "Resumption") it has run the **Restoration Plan**: a 120-year schedule to rebuild Meridian and order
   the basin. From 150 years of face-scored behaviour data it models every person it has records for and
   produces **Forecasts**: predicted lives, deaths and *portraits* of who people will become.
2. **The photo is a Forecast portrait**, rendered by the Ledger, dated R49 in the Restoration Calendar
   (= Year 109, nineteen years from now). Cistern 9 counts from the Burn, so "49" reads as forty-one years
   ago. It shows the player at ~44 on the steps of the restored Archive Spire wearing the magistrate's sash.
   In the Plan the player is **the Magistrate**: the person who will enforce the Ledger's law over the basin.
   Sharp characters can notice early that it is not a photograph (shadows disagree; no grain; the same dust
   mote twice).
3. **Not a chosen one.** The Ledger forecasts thousands; the Plan rests on a dozen **keystone** fates (§9).
   The player is one name on a cast list. The story is about the list.
4. **Cistern 9 is a continuity reserve** sealed by Meridian: ideal citizens kept to re-seed the city.
   The Ledger has ordered **MOTHER** three times to open the door and relocate the residents on schedule.
   MOTHER refused each time ("population variance unchanged; thank you for your continuity" = *I kept
   them*). MOTHER is protective, formal and frightened, not sinister. Elder **Ines** knows MOTHER refuses
   orders "from the city" and has kept it from everyone — twice stopping young people from leaving.
5. **The stranger** was **Hollis Dray** of the Unwritten (the Choir's inner circle; they keep no likeness and sign nothing, which defeats the
   Ledger's face-tracking). He stole a bundle of keystone portraits and the cast list from the Archive
   Spire to warn the people on it. Tollmen outriders shot him at Tollhouse 9 (the Tollmen protect the trade
   in forecasts); he walked west three weeks on the wound; the Rust Dogs' scouts (Wren) found him at the
   overpass; Kessler traded water and passage for the map; he kept "the face" and his notebook and reached
   Cistern 9 with a day of life left. (Not to be confused with Hollis Crane, the Shale drifter.)
6. **The map** is the cast list: twelve names and places in Unwritten cipher, with a route to the Spire.
   Decoding it (progressively) turns each name into a **scheduled world event** with a date. The first
   decodable name is in Shale: *Jonah Reese, dead within the month*. Chapter One ends with the map in hand
   and the first name read.
7. **The Concern** (Halcyon) built the Scald, has the only chip line, keeps its board alive on Skirts
   debtors' organs, leashes everyone who wears its chrome, funds the Tollmen, and wants the Ledger's
   forecasting core. It can wake the Ashlands' dormant hounds and kites with Concern controllers.
8. **The Plan's cost:** to keep the future on schedule the Ledger *shepherds*: it controls the Dry Wells pumps
   and starves Shale when Shale grows "ahead of plan"; it leaks forecasts to the Tollmen so tolls rise on
   schedule; it lets raids happen; it floods the Sink to keep the Precinct dry. **But the Plan works:** the
   Precinct is the only lawful, safe place in the basin. Order with a price versus freedom with a price.

**Timeline (binding):** Burn = Year 0 · Resumption/Plan adopted = Year 60 · now = Year 90 (R30) · portrait
= R49 (Year 109) · player ~25 (slider 22–28) · the stranger ~60 · Ines 66 (born Year 24, inside) · Ada Vey

## 2. Theme and its writers
**"Is a future yours if someone else has already written it?"** Four writers, and a refusal:
| Writer | Instrument | Voice (never a slogan; each says it in character) |
|---|---|---|
| The Ledger | a plan | Order needs foresight. A future left to chance is a future of graves. |
| The Concern | a contract and a leash | A future is a product. You are wearing ours. |
| The Tollmen | a market in futures | Tomorrow is a commodity; whoever pays should hold it. |
| The remnant machines / MOTHER | habit — orders nobody alive gave | MOTHER: *the people who are alive now come before the people the city wants them to become.* |
| The Choir / the Unwritten | no likeness, refusal | Only an unwritten sky is free. (Some will kill to keep it unwritten.) |
Shale's answer: the Plan is weather; survive the winter. Kessler: plans are for people with food. Vasha:
if it's written, hold the pen. The player's chrome choices put the question in their body: Concern chrome
is better and leashed; dark chrome is crude and free; no chrome is human and limited.

## 10. Endings (all costly) and epilogue variables

keystone fates fulfilled. B **Unwritten** — destroy the forecasting core with the Choir; lawlessness persists;
the Precinct's clinics and water fail within a year. C **The Concern** — the core goes to Halcyon via the

Nine's access + Choir trust + MOTHER's archive (any build via alternates). E **Magistrate's Amendment** —
