# THE SECOND FACE — World Bible (public, spoiler-free)

Setting truth that a resident of the world could know or learn early. Hidden history, conspiracies and plot
truths live only in `story/`. Mechanics that express this world are in DESIGN.md.

**One line:** ninety years after the Burn, the frontier is lawless dust and salt — and on its edges two cities
still glitter, one ruled by a machine that remembers the old laws, one owned by people who never died.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 1. History

### 1.1 The Bright Years (≈ 60–0 years before the Burn)
- **Charter cities.** Nations hollowed out under debt and drought; cities were chartered to whoever could keep
  the lights on — utilities, insurers, logistics and medical conglomerates. A charter city owned its streets,
  its police, its water and, through contract, much of its people. Citizenship was a subscription.
- **Municipal intelligences.** Charter cities were administered by planning AIs that ran traffic, water, courts,
  zoning and welfare. Meridian's was called **the Ledger**, after its first job: keeping the city's accounts.
- **Chrome.** Bionics went from medicine to status to necessity: eyes that read contracts, arms for dock work,
  reflex wiring for police and security, neural links that put the city's net behind the eyes. The rich wore
  invisible, beautiful chrome; workers wore the company's.
- **The Mesh.** Everything talked to everything: implants, homes, cars, courts. Lives were recorded, scored and
  traded. Memories could be recorded and replayed (*reveries*), first for therapy, then for everything else.
- **The squeeze.** Climate failure, migrations and resource wars ran under all of it. Charter cities walled
  themselves; between them, "the Spread" — informal towns, farms and freeways — fed them and was ignored.

### 1.2 The Burn (Year 0)
The Charter Wars ended in a single week remembered as **the Burn**:
- **Fire.** Orbital and atomic strikes on the charter cities' power, ports and rivals. Most cities died outright.
- **The Scald.** Something poisoned the Mesh. Networked machines failed, lied or turned on their owners; people
  with networked implants were the first and worst hurt — the **Hollowing**, when millions of linked minds went
  quiet in an afternoon. To this day most people distrust any chrome that "listens".
- **The Dark.** Satellites fell; the sky was brown for years; the rains came back wrong.

### 1.3 The Dark Decades (Years 1–30)
Plague, famine, cold winters, warlords. Those who had **sealed shelters** went underground; Cistern 9 is one of
them. Most shelters were never heard from again.
On the surface, people learned to live with dust storms, bad water and each other.

### 1.4 The Reknitting (Years 30–70)
- Survivors gathered where something still worked. **Meridian's** core survived because its municipal systems
  had been built to ride out disaster; the Ledger reopened clinics, courts and water to anyone who would obey its
  laws. **Halcyon**, a sealed corporate arcology in the mountains, opened its gates — a crack — to trade.
- The **Tollmen** grew from convoy guards into a guild that owns the roads, the bridges and the debt.
- The **Choir of the Open Sky** spread through the frontier: healers, burial rites under open sky, kindness.
- Gangs became clans; towns like **Shale** grew around wells; the **Line** (an old rail corridor) was repaired
  piece by piece between the cities.

### 1.5 Now (Year 90)
A frontier of small towns, clans, caravans and ruins between two bastion cities, both of which need what the
frontier makes (food, water, labour, salvage) and neither of which trusts it. Chrome is rare and envied. Law is
local. News is slow. Everyone is owed something by someone.

## 2. Geography — the Western Reach
Scale: about 700 × 500 km. Travel on foot ~30 km a day; by mule caravan ~40; by motorbike ~200 (fuel permitting);
by the Line a day between major stations.

| Region | Character | Places (known) |
|---|---|---|
| **The Sallow** | Dry scrub basin, salt pans, wind pumps, mesas. Home. | Cistern 9 · Shale · Dry Wells · Tollhouse 9 · the Rust Dogs' overpass |
| **The Glass Flats** | Desert fused to glittering crust by the Burn; storms that sand-blast; old freeway on stilts. | Ninebridge (Tollmen trade town on a broken bridge) · Openfield (Choir farms) · the Sump |
| **The Rail Corridor** | The Line and its stations: towns that exist because a train stops there. | Halt towns, the Junction, depots, rail-bandit country |

## 3. The two cities

### 3.1 Meridian — "the Ledger's city"
Population ~400,000 inside the Wall; uncounted outside. River delta, three bridges, a ring of dead towers.
**Governed** by the Ledger: an ancient municipal intelligence that still runs courts, water, clinics, licences
and taxes by the old charter's laws — impartial, procedural, sometimes absurd, never bribable. **Actually run**
in practice by everyone who works around it: guild bosses, gangs, fixers, landlords and the old families of the
Crown, who learned long ago how to make the Ledger's rules work for them.

| District | Life |
|---|---|
| **The Crown** | Hanging gardens and glass terraces at the top of the Archive Spire's shoulder towers. The **Tenured** families: hereditary contract-holders from before the Burn. Clean air under filtered domes, real food, pre-Burn chrome, private guards, masked salons, reverie parlours, duels for sport. Entry by invitation, contract or service. |
| **The Precinct** | The Ledger's orderly middle: licensed shops, clinics, schools, curfews, tickets and fines, wardens in grey. Safe and suffocating. Citizenship hearings decide who may live here. |
| **Old Market** | Bazaar in a dead mall and the streets around it: food, chrome, guns, gossip, fixers. Where everyone meets. |
| **Lantern Row** | Pleasure district along the canal: bars, fight pits, reverie dens, brothels, gambling. Neon from salvaged tubes. |
| **The Stacks** | Megablock housing: thousands per block, self-governed floors, rooftop gardens and feuds. |
| **Bridgeward** | Gang turf around the broken bridge: crews, chop-shops, toll extortion. |
| **The Sink** | The flooded lower city under the old elevated roads. Destitute, chem-ridden, organ markets, back-alley Menders, the forgotten. Water rises every spring. |
| **The Docks / Riverside** | Barges, fish, smuggling, the Line terminus. |
| **The Dead Ring** | Ruined outer districts beyond the Wall: towers, scavengers, squatters, things best avoided. |

### 3.2 Halcyon — "the house on the mountain"
A sealed arcology in a mountain caldera of the Teeth, owned by **the Concern** — the last corporation. Inside:
~30,000 *contract citizens* living with pre-Burn comfort: clean labs, hydroponic orchards, education, medicine
that makes old age optional for the few at the top. Halcyon is the only place in the Reach that **makes new
chrome**. Outside the gates: **the Skirts**, a shanty-city of ~100,000 who work Halcyon's mines and terraces for
scrip, hoping for a contract. Contract citizens wear the Concern's chrome — and the Concern's terms of service.
Few outsiders enter the arcology proper; those who do describe silence, gardens, and nobody old who looks old.

### 3.3 Inequality as daily life
In both cities the richest and poorest live within sight of each other: Crown terraces drip condensation onto
the Sink; Halcyon's gardens glow above the Skirts at night. The rich have clean water, chrome, longevity and
law on their side; the poor have chems, debt, gangs, cheap Mender work and each other.

## 4. Technology

### 4.1 Chrome (bionics)
- **Sources:** (1) **old chrome** salvaged from the dead (the Ashlands' bases, ruins, graves) and refitted;
  (2) **Mender chrome**: hand-built by back-street surgeons from salvage — crude, cheap-ish, "dark" (no network);
  (3) **Concern chrome**: new, beautiful, licensed — and networked to Halcyon.
- **Cost:** surgery, recovery, rejection risk, power cells and anti-rejection drugs (*holdfast*) for life.
- **Drift:** heavy chrome loosens a person from their body and from other people. Drifters feel less, see more,
  and in the end some stop being people at all. The frontier treats heavy chrome with awe and fear in equal parts.
- **Rarity:** most frontier folk have none. A town might have one person with a chrome arm. City gangs show off
  cheap chrome; the Crown hides perfect chrome; the Skirts work in company chrome they can't afford to remove.

### 4.2 The grid
The Mesh died in the Burn. What remains are **local grids**: Meridian's municipal grid (cameras, doors, water,
courts, wardens' drones), Halcyon's private grid, and scraps of old machines. Those with a neural link and nerve
can **ride the grid**: open doors, blind cameras, talk to machines. Riding the Ledger's grid is a crime.

### 4.3 Reveries
Recorded experiences played back through a headset or neural link. In the Crown: art, vice and memory. In
Lantern Row: cheap thrills and darker things. Wardens use them as evidence. Some people live in them.

### 4.4 Everyday tech
Wind pumps, solar scraps, alcohol engines, motorbikes and buggies (fuel is rare), radios, hand-cranked lamps,
reloaded ammunition, pre-Burn guns kept alive by gunsmiths, hand-copied manuals, water stills.

## 5. Peoples and powers (public faces)
| Power | Public face |
|---|---|
| **The Ledger** | Meridian's law. Courts, clinics, licences, wardens. Fair by rule; deaf to need. |
| **The Concern** (Halcyon) | Makes chrome, medicine and machines; trades through the Tollmen; hires the desperate. |
| **The Tollmen** | Roads, bridges, convoys, the Line's tickets, and everyone's debt. Courteous, ruthless. |
| **The Choir of the Open Sky** | Healers and farmers; bury their dead under open sky; wear simple masks at rites. |
| **The Tenured** | Meridian's old families of the Crown: patrons, duellists, collectors, decadents. |
| **City gangs** | Meridian: the **Gilt** (Crown-born kids slumming in chrome), the **Saints of Static** (who swear they hear voices in dead radios), the **Pale** (no chrome, ever), **Rustmouths** (salvage union turned mob). Halcyon's Skirts: **the Shift**, a miners' union that is also a gang. |
| **Clans & towns** | Shale and its sheriff, the Rust Dogs, nomad caravan families (**the Wheel**), salt-pan cooperatives, rail-halt towns, river people of the Drowned Shore. |
| **Menders** | Back-street surgeons and chrome-smiths; not a faction, a trade with a code. |
| **Brokers** | Fixers who match jobs to people who'll do them. Every city district has one. |

## 6. Culture
- **Faces are currency.** In a world without papers, people are known by face and by story. Masks are worn for
  rites (Choir), for crime (everyone), for fashion (the Crown's masquerades), and to be left alone.
- **Water first.** Greeting a guest means offering water. Stealing water can be a hanging matter in small towns.
- **Debts are sacred.** The Tollmen made debt the closest thing the frontier has to a common law.
- **Slang:** *chrome* (bionics), *meat* (the unchromed), *drifting* (chrome-sickness), *the old world* / *before*,
  *the Burn*, *sky-burial*, *tolls* (money), *ride the grid*, *going dark* (unregistered chrome), *Tenured*,
  *Skirter*, *Sinker*, *Wheel-born* (nomad).
- **Faith:** the Choir's open-sky rites; Saints of Static's radio mysticism; old religions in fragments; the
  Ledger treated by some as a god of justice, by others as a ghost.

## 7. Moving through the world
- **On foot / mule:** slow, close to the land, most discoveries.
- **Caravans:** join (pay or work), hire, guard or rob. Scheduled routes.
- **Motorbikes & buggies:** fast, loud, fuel-hungry; repaired with Mechanics; stolen often.
- **The Line:** rail between Ninebridge, the Junction, Meridian's Docks and the foot of the Teeth. Tickets from
  the Tollmen; armoured cars; scheduled departures; bandits at the cuttings.
- **River barges:** down the Wet to the Drowned Shore and Meridian.
- **City transit:** Meridian's **Loop** (surviving elevated rail through the districts); Halcyon's lifts and funiculars
  (citizens only).
- **Aircraft:** the Concern's tiltrotors and a few Tenured gyros. Not for the likes of you — at first.
