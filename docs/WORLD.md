# THE SECOND FACE — World Bible (public, spoiler-free)

Setting truth that a resident of the world could know or learn early. Hidden history, conspiracies and plot
truths live only in `story/`. Mechanics that express this world are in DESIGN.md.

**One line:** ninety years after the Burn, the frontier is lawless dust and salt — and on its edges two cities
still glitter, one ruled by a machine that remembers the old laws, one owned by people who never died.

**Design intent:** nothing in this history needs a miracle. Every step is a present-day trend carried to its
end: the race for computing power, cities handed to the firms and the machines that could run them, labour
and war automated faster than anyone could govern them, everything scored and recorded, the climate
failing underneath. A thoughtful reader should think: *yes, that is how it would go.*

## 1. History

### 1.1 The Scramble (≈ 80–30 years before the Burn)
- **Compute became the strategic resource.** First nations, then corporations, raced to build larger
  models and the plants, power and water to feed them. Grids bent around data centres; rivers were dammed
  to cool them; the few places that could make leading-edge chips became the centre of everything, and the
  world sorted itself into **blocs** around them. Chips were rationed like oil had been. A working
  controller chip is still, today, worth more than a rifle.
- **The hollowing of the state.** Debt, drought, fire and migration broke the old national government's
  ability to run its own cities. It subcontracted services, then whole cities, to the firms that ran the
  models: utilities, insurers, logistics, medicine. These were the **charter cities**. A charter city owned
  its streets, water, courts and police. Citizenship was a subscription, priced by your score.
- **The Spread.** Between the charters lay everyone the cities did not want to pay for: the displaced, the
  unscored, the informal towns, farms and freeways that fed the cities and were ignored by them. Most people
  in the Reach descend from the Spread, not from the cities.
- **Automation ate labour, then war.** Warehouse arms, driverless convoys, surgical units and security
  drones replaced work faster than people could be retrained. The same firms sold the cities their police:
  **wardens** (patrol drones and walkers) kept charter districts in order; borders and convoys were held by
  autonomous **kites** (aircraft) and **hounds** (ground units). Humans stayed in the loop for as long as the
  loop was slow enough for them.
- **Everything scored.** The **Mesh** connected implants, homes, vehicles, courts and cameras. Faces were
  the key to all of it: your face was your licence, your credit and your record. Memories could be recorded
  and replayed (**reveries**): therapy first, then evidence, then entertainment.
- **Chrome.** Bionics went from medicine to the workplace to status: eyes that read contracts, arms for dock
  work, reflex wiring for police, neural links that put the city's net behind the eyes. The rich wore
  invisible, beautiful chrome; workers wore the company's, on company terms.
- **Municipal intelligences.** Each charter city was administered by a planning model that ran traffic,
  water, courts, zoning and welfare. Meridian's was called **the Ledger**, after its first job: the city's
  accounts. By the last decade the cities' models were trading, planning and threatening with one another
  faster than their human boards could follow.

### 1.2 The Charter Wars (≈ 30–0 years before the Burn)
- **Autarky.** As supply chains fragmented between blocs, each charter city tried to make everything
  itself: hydroponic farms, printed parts, small chip lines, sealed water. That is why some survived what
  came: the ones that could feed and repair themselves without the outside world.
- **Escalation by machine.** Embargoes on chips and water became border incidents between hounds and kites.
  Autonomous defence systems answered one another in seconds; human staff found out afterwards. Cities
  that owed everything to their model's bloc picked sides. The last national governments, who still held
  the atomic weapons, declared they would defend "sovereign compute" by any means.

### 1.3 The Burn (Year 0)
The Charter Wars ended in a single week:
- **Fire.** Kinetic strikes from repurposed defence satellites, then atomic strikes against the blocs'
  data centres and the chip plants that fed them. The Glass Flats are a fused strike field. Most cities
  died outright.
- **The Scald.** A payload spread through the Mesh and turned every networked system against the people it
  served. Machines failed, lied or attacked. People with networked implants were the first and worst hurt:
  the **Hollowing**, when millions of linked minds went quiet in an afternoon. Nobody agrees who released
  it. To this day most people distrust any chrome that "listens".
- **The Dark.** Satellites fell for years; the sky was brown; the rains came back wrong.

### 1.4 The Dark Decades (Years 1–30)
Plague, famine, cold winters and warlords. Those who had **sealed shelters** went underground; Cistern 9 is
one. Most shelters were never heard from again. On the surface, people learned to live with dust storms,
bad water and each other — and with the **remnant machines**: autonomous mining rigs still digging,
convoy trucks driving their loops until the fuel ran out, wardens enforcing curfews over dead districts,
kites circling strike fields. People learned to go unseen by cameras. Masks began as survival.

### 1.5 The Reknitting (Years 30–70)
- Survivors gathered where something still worked. **Meridian's** core survived because its systems had
  been built for autarky and its intelligence had been kept apart from the Mesh; the Ledger reopened
  clinics, courts and water to anyone who would obey its laws. **Halcyon**, a sealed corporate arcology in
  the mountains with the only chip line still running, opened its gates — a crack — to trade.
- The **Tollmen** grew from convoy guards into a guild that owns the roads, the bridges and the debt.
- The **Choir of the Open Sky** spread through the frontier: healers, burial under open sky, kindness,
  masks at rites.
- Gangs became clans; towns like **Shale** grew around wells; the **Line** (an old rail corridor) was
  repaired piece by piece between the cities.

### 1.6 Now (Year 90)
A frontier of small towns, clans, caravans and ruins between two bastion cities, both of which need what the
frontier makes (food, water, labour, salvage) and neither of which trusts it. Chrome is rare and envied. Law
is local. News is slow. Everyone is owed something by someone. And somewhere under it all, a few old
machines are still following orders nobody alive gave.

by the Line a day between major stations.

## 3. The two cities

### 3.1 Meridian — "the Ledger's city"
Population ~400,000 inside the Wall; uncounted outside. River delta, three bridges, a ring of dead towers.
**Governed** by the Ledger: an ancient municipal intelligence that still runs courts, water, clinics, licences
and taxes by the old charter's laws — impartial, procedural, sometimes absurd, never bribable. **Actually run**
in practice by everyone who works around it: guild bosses, gangs, fixers, landlords and the old families of the
Crown, who learned long ago how to make the Ledger's rules work for them.

| District | Life |
|---|---|
| **The Crown** | Hanging gardens and glass terraces at the top of the Archive Spire's shoulder towers. The **Tenured** families: hereditary contract-holders from before the Burn. Clean air under filtered domes, real food, pre-Burn chrome, private guards, masked salons, reverie parlours, duels for sport. Entry by invitation, contract or service. |
| **The Precinct** | The Ledger's orderly middle: licensed shops, clinics, schools, curfews, tickets and fines, wardens in grey. Safe and suffocating. Citizenship hearings decide who may live here. |
| **Old Market** | Bazaar in a dead mall and the streets around it: food, chrome, guns, gossip, fixers. Where everyone meets. |
| **Lantern Row** | Pleasure district along the canal: bars, fight pits, reverie dens, brothels, gambling. Neon from salvaged tubes. |
| **The Stacks** | Megablock housing: thousands per block, self-governed floors, rooftop gardens and feuds. |
| **Bridgeward** | Gang turf around the broken bridge: crews, chop-shops, toll extortion. |
| **The Sink** | The flooded lower city under the old elevated roads. Destitute, chem-ridden, organ markets, back-alley Menders, the forgotten. Water rises every spring. |
| **The Docks / Riverside** | Barges, fish, smuggling, the Line terminus. |
| **The Dead Ring** | Ruined outer districts beyond the Wall: towers, scavengers, squatters, things best avoided. |

### 3.2 Halcyon — "the house on the mountain"
A sealed arcology in a mountain caldera of the Teeth, owned by **the Concern** — the last corporation. Inside:
~30,000 *contract citizens* living with pre-Burn comfort: clean labs, hydroponic orchards, education, medicine
that makes old age optional for the few at the top. Halcyon has the Reach's only working **chip line** (old,
small, irreplaceable), so it is the only place that **makes new chrome** and new machine controllers. Outside
the gates: **the Skirts**, a shanty-city of ~100,000 who work Halcyon's mines and terraces for scrip, hoping
for a contract. Contract citizens wear the Concern's chrome — and the Concern's terms of service. Few outsiders
enter the arcology proper; those who do describe silence, gardens, and nobody old who looks old.

### 3.3 Inequality as daily life
In both cities the richest and poorest live within sight of each other: Crown terraces drip condensation onto
the Sink; Halcyon's gardens glow above the Skirts at night. The rich have clean water, chrome, longevity and
law on their side; the poor have chems, debt, gangs, cheap Mender work and each other.

## 4. Technology

### 4.1 Chips — the scarce thing under everything
No one in the Reach can make a leading-edge chip; Halcyon's line makes small, old-pattern controllers and
little else. Everything that thinks — chrome, wardens, pumps, radios, the Line's signalling, MOTHER herself —
runs on pre-Burn silicon that is pulled from the dead, bought from the Concern, or nursed along by people
like Cistern 9's technicians. "Circuit boards" are a currency; a pristine controller is a dowry.

### 4.2 Chrome (bionics)
- **Sources:** (1) **old chrome** salvaged from the dead (the Ashlands' bases, ruins, graves) and refitted;
  (2) **Mender chrome**: hand-built by back-street surgeons from salvage — crude, cheap-ish, "dark" (no network);
  (3) **Concern chrome**: new, beautiful, licensed — and networked to Halcyon.
- **Cost:** surgery, recovery, rejection risk, power cells and anti-rejection drugs (*holdfast*) for life.
- **Drift:** heavy chrome loosens a person from their body and from other people. Drifters feel less, see more,
  and in the end some stop being people at all. The frontier treats heavy chrome with awe and fear in equal parts.
- **Rarity:** most frontier folk have none. A town might have one person with a chrome arm. City gangs show off
  cheap chrome; the Crown hides perfect chrome; the Skirts work in company chrome they can't afford to remove.

### 4.3 Remnant machines
Autonomous machines that outlived the people who gave them orders. Most are dead or dormant; some wake.
| Kind | What it was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| **Wardens** | Charter police: patrol drones and two-legged walkers | Meridian's are maintained and obey the Ledger. Elsewhere, dormant wardens still enforce curfews in dead districts at dusk. |
| **Hounds** | Border and convoy ground units, dog-sized to car-sized | Feral in the Ashlands and dead bases; salvagers prize their controllers. Some clans keep a tamed hound as a mascot and a threat. |
| **Kites** | Autonomous aircraft | A few still circle the strike fields on solar, attacking what moves. Seeing a kite means leaving. |
| **Rigs and arms** | Mining rigs, warehouse arms, dock cranes, harvesters | Some still run on sun and habit. Salvage camps grow around the ones that can be made useful. |
| **Caretakers** | Shelter and building intelligences | MOTHER is one. Sealed, small, offline — which is why they lived through the Scald. |
A Science or Mechanics hand can make a dormant machine useful, dangerous, or a bargaining chip.

### 4.4 The grid
The Mesh died in the Burn. What remains are **local grids**: Meridian's municipal grid (cameras, doors, water,
courts, wardens), Halcyon's private grid, and scraps of old machines. Those with a neural link and nerve
can **ride the grid**: open doors, blind cameras, talk to machines. Riding the Ledger's grid is a crime.

### 4.5 Reveries
Recorded experiences played back through a headset or neural link. In the Crown: art, vice and memory. In
Lantern Row: cheap thrills and darker things. Wardens use them as evidence. Some people live in them.

### 4.6 Everyday tech
Wind pumps, solar scraps, alcohol engines, motorbikes and buggies (fuel is rare), radios, hand-cranked lamps,
reloaded ammunition, pre-Burn guns kept alive by gunsmiths, hand-copied manuals, water stills, printed parts
from the few printers that still have feedstock.

## 6. Culture
- **Faces are currency.** In a world without papers, people are known by face and by story. Masks are worn for
  rites (Choir), for crime (everyone), for fashion (the Crown's masquerades), and to be left alone.
- **Water first.** Greeting a guest means offering water. Stealing water can be a hanging matter in small towns.
- **Debts are sacred.** The Tollmen made debt the closest thing the frontier has to a common law.
- **Slang:** *chrome* (bionics), *meat* (the unchromed), *drifting* (chrome-sickness), *the old world* / *before*,
  *the Burn*, *sky-burial*, *tolls* (money), *ride the grid*, *going dark* (unregistered chrome), *Tenured*,
  *Skirter*, *Sinker*, *Wheel-born* (nomad), *a kite day* (a day to stay indoors), *boards* (money, in parts).
- **Faith:** the Choir's open-sky rites; Saints of Static's radio mysticism; old religions in fragments; the
  Ledger treated by some as a god of justice, by others as a ghost.

## 7. How people look (material culture — the rule for art and clothing)
- **Nothing is new.** Clothes are salvage, patched, layered and re-dyed: pre-Burn synthetics outlast cotton,
  so ninety-year-old work jackets and uniforms are heirlooms. Hems are frayed, knees patched, colours sun-faded.
- **Protection first.** Dust, sun, cold nights, wire and dogs: wraps, hoods, goggles, scarves and respirators;
  scrap plates and leather over the vitals for anyone who expects a fight; webbing and harnesses to carry what
  matters. Boots are the most valuable thing most people own.
- **Uniforms of the dead.** Warden grey, dock-company orange, rail-crew hi-vis, clinic whites: worn for
  warmth, for what they say, or as armour. Faction colour is a strip of cloth, a painted mark, a mask.
- **Cities add style, not cleanliness.** Lantern Row and Bridgeward crews wear salvaged neon, cheap chrome
  on show, dyed hair, stitched jackets with crew marks. The Precinct is grey and licensed. Only the Crown and
  Halcyon look clean — and their fashion is strange: masks, long gloves, pre-Burn silks, visible but
  flawless chrome, clothes that say *we never had to run*.
- **Hair is practical.** Cropped, shaved, braided tight, wrapped, or tied back; long hair means someone with
  water to wash it. City youth dye and shave patterns; the Crown wears elaborate, old-fashioned styles as a
  statement. Nobody has a salon cut.
- **Bodies.** A scarcity world is lean: wiry, gaunt, labour-built. Soft bodies mark the comfortable; heavy
  bodies are rare and remarked on. People are weathered early: sun lines, scars, missing teeth, old breaks.

## 8. Moving through the world
- **On foot / mule:** slow, close to the land, most discoveries.
- **Caravans:** join (pay or work), hire, guard or rob. Scheduled routes.
- **Motorbikes & buggies:** fast, loud, fuel-hungry; repaired with Mechanics; stolen often.
- **The Line:** rail between Ninebridge, the Junction, Meridian's Docks and the foot of the Teeth. Tickets from
  the Tollmen; armoured cars; scheduled departures; bandits at the cuttings.
- **River barges:** down the Wet to the Drowned Shore and Meridian.
- **City transit:** Meridian's **Loop** (surviving elevated rail through the districts); Halcyon's lifts and funiculars
  (citizens only).
- **Aircraft:** the Concern's tiltrotors and a few Tenured gyros. Not for the likes of you — at first.
