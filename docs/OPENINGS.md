# Openings: what the great ones share, and the rules for ours (spoiler-free)

The owner's note (2026-10-29, binding, CLAUDE.md §2): the player is not a shelter-dweller stepping out for the first
time. The opening must give the player character a life and the people in it, so the player cares about them as much
as about the hero, and it must build the world, establish those characters and train the mechanics at once. This file
studies how landmark games open and turns what they share into rules. Our own story's opening is designed in
`story/` (secret); nothing here says what happens in it.

## 1. The openings studied

| Game | The opening | What it does at once |
|---|---|---|
| **Kingdom Come: Deliverance** (2018) | Henry runs errands in his village: collect a debt, buy charcoal, fetch beer for his father, fight the neighbour's boys. A raid burns the village; his parents die. | Every system (talk, trade, fight, sneak) is taught by an errand for a named person; the village is known street by street before it burns; the motive is grief, not a quest marker. |
| **Red Dead Redemption 2** (2018) | The gang shelters in a blizzard after a robbery gone wrong. You hunt with an old friend, ride out to bring a lost member home, rob a train with the others. | The gang is a family introduced through chores done together; mechanics come from the person beside you; the gang's predicament is the plot. |
| **The Witcher 3** (2015) | A dream of the old keep: the hero trains a girl he raised, under his own mentor's eye. Then a small war-torn village is the first open area. | The tutorial *is* the relationship that drives the game; the first open area is contained, with local problems that teach the world's rules before the big map. |
| **Horizon Zero Dawn** (2017) | An outcast child raised by an outcast mentor; years pass in a few scenes of training; a coming-of-age trial; a massacre; the mentor dies protecting her. | The mentor's love is built by doing things with him; the trial teaches combat; the loss turns the personal question into the journey. |
| **The Last of Us** (2013) | The first minutes are played as the hero's daughter on an ordinary evening; the collapse; her death. | The player inhabits the person the hero loses; one loss sets the tone and the hero's later choices. |
| **God of War** (2018) | A father and son fell a tree for a funeral pyre, then set out to scatter her ashes. | Grief and a promise set the destination; the son's lessons are the tutorials; their relationship is the story. |
| **Cyberpunk 2077** (2020) | A life path (nomad, street kid, corporate) prologue, then a friendship built over a montage and a job that ends in the friend's death. | The player's chosen past is a place with its own people; the bond is shown, not told; the friend's loss is the hinge. |
| **Dragon Age: Origins** (2009) | Six origin stories, each an hour or two in the hero's home with family and rivals, each ending in betrayal or loss. | The chosen origin is a home with people who come back later; the choice of origin is a choice of whom you lose. |
| **Mass Effect** (2007) | Walk the ship and meet the crew, then a routine mission goes wrong on a colony world. | The crew is met as people before they are a party; the hook implicates the hero personally. |
| **Final Fantasy VII** (1997) | A sabotage raid with a small resistance cell in a city that feeds on its own poor. | A team, a cause and a city's injustice are shown in action in the first hour. |
| **Breath of the Wild** (2017) | A walled plateau: four trials, an old man, the whole world visible from its cliffs. | A contained space teaches every system one at a time; leaving it feels earned. |
| **Half-Life** (1998) | A tram ride to work through the facility, small talk, a routine experiment that fails. | Ordinary work and colleagues first; the world explained by watching it run; the disaster comes out of the job. |

## 2. What they share (the common denominators)

1. **A home before the road.** The hero starts somewhere ordinary to them (a village, a camp, a keep, a ship) with a
   routine and a trade. It is contained, so the player learns it street by street.
2. **People before plot.** Three to six people with their own wants are met by doing things with them or for them
   (a mentor, family, a friend, a rival). The player has helped, disappointed or argued with each before anything
   goes wrong.
3. **Every tutorial is a relationship beat.** A system is first used on an errand for someone (trade for the parent,
   hunt with the friend, spar with the mentor, sneak with the rival), never in a void with a panel.
4. **The world through particulars.** Prices, rules, rumours, jobs and quarrels show how the world works; nobody
   explains its history unasked.
5. **The rupture comes from the world's real forces,** and it lands on the people the player has just come to know (a
   raid, a death, a betrayal, a debt). The hero leaves for a personal reason.
6. **The mystery implicates the hero personally,** and only after the attachment exists.
7. **An early choice that echoes.** Something decided in the first hour (what to say, whom to side with, what to give
   up) is remembered and returns, by those people, much later.
8. **A reason to come back.** Home is still there, changed, and its people are part of the later story (letters,
   visits, a home in danger at the climax).
9. **Pacing.** 45–90 minutes; one system at a time; the release into the open world is a moment.
10. **What to avoid:** amnesia; a chosen one; emerging from a sealed bunker or vault; a hero with no ties; a kidnapped
    child or spouse as the whole motive (it is a genre cliché and close to a well-known franchise's).

## 3. Rules for The Second Face's opening

- **Home.** The player character has a life before the game: a home in the Reach with a trade, a routine and a place in
  it (the four origins are four trades in that home, each teaching its own system and carrying its own thread).
- **The people.** At least five named people the player character is bound to (family, a mentor, a friend, a rival, a
  voice they have known all their life), each with a want of their own, each met in a scene that teaches a system.
  Each returns later: in letters, in news, in person, and at the end.
- **Chores that teach.** Barter, talk with its checks, scavenging and the pack, sneaking, a first fight, the camp
  (cook, mend, write), the notebook and its leads: each first used for one of those people.
- **The world shown.** Water, Tolls, the toll road, the forecasts' first trace, the machines, the faces: in prices,
  quarrels and jobs, not lectures.
- **The stranger arrives into that life.** The dying stranger and the photograph come after the player cares about the
  home and its people, and what follows puts those people at risk; the player character leaves for their sake as
  much as for the question.
- **A choice that echoes.** The opening asks at least one hard choice that its people remember into Act 3.
- **A home to come back to.** The home and its people are part of the climax.
- **Length.** About an hour; contained; one system at a time; leaving is the first open-world moment.
- **Continuity.** The known facts stand (the Burn, Cistern 9 and MOTHER, the dying stranger, the photograph of the
  player older in a ruined city dated 41 years ago, Shale, Chapter One ending with the stranger's map); the player
  character's place in them changes. The full design and the content changes it needs are in
  `story/STORY_PLAN_SECRET.md` (§18) and, spoiler-free, in `docs/BUILD_PLAN_V1.md` (P7).
